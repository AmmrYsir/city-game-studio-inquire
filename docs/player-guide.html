1How your review score is decided

When your game ships, 4 magazines review it. Their score comes from four things:

🎯

Pick the RIGHT genre + platform + audience

The game grades three pairings and multiplies them: Excellent ×1.10 Good ×1.05 OK ×1.00 Poor ×0.85

genre↔platform · theme↔genre · platform↔target audience. One "Poor" quietly costs 15% of your score. The in-game match tips are real coded data — trust them.

⚖️

Balance your capacity sliders

Every genre has a secret "shopping list" (section 2). Reviewers grade the proportions of your spending, not the total. Matching proportions = full marks (28 points × 3 phases). Lopsided allocation can cost half the capacity score and triggers "too much X" / "not enough X" complaints.

⏱️

Ship on time

Dev time is worth up to 20 points, only inside the healthy window. Indie 12–24 wks · Normal 24–56 · Big 48–96 · AAA 72–144. Every week past the max steals points back.

🔁

Don't repeat yourself

Same theme+genre combo within ~6–12 months = up to −25% score.

💡 Score caps: Indie max 85 · Normal 90 · Big 95 · AAA 100. A bloated engine lowers the cap further.

3Pricing — the sweet spot is bigger than you think

🏷️

How price actually affects sales

The game compares your price to your size's max price:

  • At or below max price → volume ratio ≈ max_price / your_price. Halving the price roughly doubles copies sold.
  • Above max price → a penalty kicks in: malus × limit / price. The malus (0.5–0.8) is brutal — pricing AAA at 150 instead of 120 costs you ~35% volume.
  • Recommendation: price at the size's average — near-max revenue without the malus. Only go below average for fanbase farming or when a game underperformed.

4Marketing & hype

📣

Where hype comes from

  • Marketing campaigns (run during the window before release): the campaign's ratio multiplies your last month's unit sales into extra launch hype.
  • Publisher / announcement hype: self-published games start at 0.5 hype ratio; using a publisher or a good announcement pushes it up to 4×.
  • Events & conventions add a static bonus, and your last month's sales add proportional hype.
  • A hired Marketing Manager multiplies all of it by their bonus.

👥

Fanbase + freshness = units

You sell a share of the platform's userbase, split with every other game released there. Fanbase adds direct sales; both fade as months pass. Games scoring below the fans' expectation lose fans monthly instead.

😱

Bad things

  • Piracy: cracked games sell ⅓ (retro platforms lack protection).
  • Engine royalties if using someone else's engine; publisher royalties if not self-published.
  • Taxes monthly — an accountant employee reduces them.
