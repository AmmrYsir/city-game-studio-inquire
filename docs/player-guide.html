Player's Guide to Great Scores & Big Sales

Everything below is read straight from the game's own code — no guessing, no myths. Here's how the game actually decides your review scores and sales.

1How your review score is decided

When your game ships, 4 game magazines each review it. Their score comes from four things — in order of importance:

🎯

Pick the RIGHT genre (biggest factor)

The game checks what kind of game you promised (genre) against the platform, the theme and the audience you picked. Each pairing gets a grade:

Excellent = ×1.10 Good = ×1.05 OK = ×1.00 Poor = ×0.85

Three of these grades multiply together, so one "Poor" pairing can quietly cost you 15% of your final score. The game shows you match tips while you pick — trust them.

⚖️

Balance your capacity sliders (the hidden test)

Every genre has a secret "shopping list" of how much Gameplay, Story, Sound, AI etc. it expects — see the table in section 2. The game doesn't just count your total points: it checks whether you spent them in the proportions the genre wants.

✅ Match the proportions → full marks (each of the 3 dev phases is worth up to 28 points, 84 total).
❌ Way over-invest in one area or starve another → up to half your capacity score can be lost, and reviewers will literally complain "too much X" / "not enough X".
⏱️

Don't drag development on too long

Dev time is worth up to 20 points — but only if you finish inside the "expected weeks" window for your game size. Every extra week past the maximum steals points back. Speed matters, but rushing below the minimum also scores poorly.

SizeHealthy dev time
Indie12–24 weeks
Normal24–56 weeks
Big48–96 weeks
AAA72–144 weeks
🔁

Don't repeat yourself

Making the same theme + genre combo again too soon gets a "seen it before" penalty — up to −25% off your score. Wait roughly 6–12 months (depending on difficulty) between sequels of the exact same combo, or mix in a different theme.

💡 Score caps: Indie games can never score above 85, Normal 90, Big 95, AAA 100. And a bloated custom engine can lower even that cap — keep your engine lean for the platform.

3The four game sizes

🕹️ Indie

Max 85
  • 1 person, no producer needed
  • 1 000 fans to unlock
  • Sells up to 100 000 copies
  • Price ~10, can push to 20
  • 3–6 months dev time

💿 Normal

Max 90
  • 5 staff
  • 10 000 fans
  • 500 000 copies
  • Price ~20, up to 50
  • 6–14 months

📦 Big

Max 95
  • 25 staff + a producer
  • 50 000 fans
  • 2.5M copies
  • Price ~40, up to 80
  • 12–24 months

🏆 AAA

Max 100
  • 100 staff + a producer
  • 100 000 fans
  • 10M copies
  • Price ~60, up to 120
  • 18–36 months

Marketing campaigns scale the same way — "Full campaign" costs 350k for indie but 5M for AAA, and multiplies your launch hype by ×3.8 vs the free campaign's ×1.05.

4How monthly sales actually work

📊

Your review score is sales dynamite

Sales don't grow linearly with the score — they explode. The game raises your score to a huge power (7.5 for indie, up to 14 for AAA). Practically:

Score 60
weak sales
Score 75
decent
Score 85
strong
Score 95
blockbuster

(indie example; the gap gets even wider on bigger sizes)

👥

Fanbase + freshness = units

Each month you sell a slice of the platform's player base, shared with every other game released there. Your fanbase adds direct sales, but divided by how many months you've been out — sales peak early and fade. Fresh platform launches and updates give a cycle boost.

🏷️

Price sweet spot

Each size has an average and a max price. Pricing near the average gives you a healthy volume bonus; pricing above max triggers a penalty that slashes volume. Cheap games sell more copies — but discounts also reduce fan gain.

📣

Hype from marketing

Campaigns and a good marketing manager add a one-time hype spike on top of your monthly units — capped by platform size. Retained players (online games) keep paying a monthly fee if your servers can handle the load.

😱

Bad things that hurt

  • Piracy: a cracked game sells ⅓ of what it should.
  • Fan loss: if your score is below what your fans expect, you literally lose fans every month — a flop on a big fanbase bleeds hard.
  • Engine royalties: using someone else's engine cuts your income.
  • Taxes take their cut every month — an accountant employee reduces them.

5The golden rules

1️⃣ Check the match tips before starting — Excellent genre/platform/theme pairings are free score.
2️⃣ Follow the genre's shopping list above — balanced allocation beats dumping everything into one slider.
3️⃣ Ship on time — the last weeks of overtime cost more than they give.
4️⃣ Never back-to-back the same combo — alternate themes or wait it out.
5️⃣ Grow into sizes — a Big game with 20k fans will flop; fanbase gates sales harder than score does.
6️⃣ A 5-point review difference is huge — near release, one more polish week often pays for itself.
